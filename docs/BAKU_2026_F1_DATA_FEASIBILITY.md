# Baku 2026 F1: event-data feasibility audit

Checked 25 September 2026 (UTC). The [official F1 schedule](https://www.formula1.com/en/racing/2026/azerbaijan) places the Azerbaijan Grand Prix on 24–26 September. Baku is outside the EU, but is included because its city-street race offers a directly relevant road-disruption case. Original notice HTML is retained only in ignored local `data/raw/`; the [tracked source-fact card](../data/event_cases/baku-f1-2026-source-facts.json) records URLs, byte hashes, timezone and unknowns without distributing third-party article text.

**Source-quality trap:** several Baku City Circuit search results show a 2026 site wrapper while their article body explicitly discusses the 2024 or 2025 race. The page title/year in the wrapper is not enough. Use the 2026 traffic-plan body above and preserve its bytes/quote spans before mapping. The transit-corridor windows on 21–23 September are already past; they cannot be sold as a prospective experiment begun on 25 September.

The local, reproducible road-only build covers `[49.78, 40.32, 49.96, 40.44]` and yields **33,035 junctions, 74,289 directed road segments, 130,326 turn connections and 805 candidate facilities**. The immutable CityPack SHA-256 is `8adba2949ca4d6637b0250ef8eb72fd6833456abcad3674dcfeb3fcfe1577c89`; KG files contain 75,095 ontology objects, 130,326 candidate directed turn links and 5,732 candidate facility-access links, with exact hashes in the audit. These are imported geometry/relationship candidates, not a circuit closure map or verified facility entrances. No GTFS was imported. Build commands and ignored-data policy are in the [data README](../data/README.md).

Name resolution already exposes a concrete mapping hazard: the bounded OSM graph has 36 directed edges named `Puşkin küçəsi` and 24 named `Sergey Senyuşkin küçəsi`. An unbounded substring match for “Pushkin”/`uşkin` could mix unrelated streets. Even exact `Puşkin küçəsi` name matching does not establish the announced extent between the two intersections, direction, or exceptions; preserve this as a candidate pool until endpoint geometry is reviewed.

## Decision and next evidence gate

- **V1a source transcription: feasible.** The 2026 circuit plan and AYNA notice offer named streets, dates, hours and route IDs. Freeze the exact source pages and timezone (`Asia/Baku`, UTC+04:00), separately recording missing end-time and exception details.
- **V1b physical reconstruction: partial.** A dated pre-onset OSM extract and a bounded, typed road KG exist, but the circuit boundary, individual directed restriction edges, turn permissions and bus access need independent mapping review. The builder's technical import pass is not V1b validation.
- **V2 observed operational impact: not established.** AYNA's diversions are a plan, and no independently archived actual operated-route/stop log was verified in this audit. A comparison to an unseen AYNA plan can score plan coverage only, with no claim of actual impact.
- **V3 numeric road prediction: blocked by evidence.** No event-hour, geolocated, directional road count/speed/travel-time series with same-hour controls and usable rights was verified. Metro daily entries could support a different, coarser outcome only after the event-day dataset arrives. Do not score road-travel-time accuracy from race results, notice text or generic current traffic.

**Practical comparison status (25 Sep 18:57 UTC):** no Baku road-segment hit, miss or false-alarm numbers exist yet. The CityPack/KG and source notices describe the road network and planned restrictions, but there is no frozen, reviewed segment-level plugin prediction paired with independent observed Baku segment data. Do not turn the 74,289 imported directed roads or the 26 announced bus detours into a prediction-accuracy denominator. The Berlin VIZ case has such an observation feed and a local GIS overlay; Baku remains a separate data-acquisition track.

The closure is already in effect, but post-closure data can still test **which streets are affected now** if an independent observed road/vehicle layer is obtained and the plugin's candidate output is fixed before looking at that layer. It cannot by itself establish the magnitude of a before/after slowdown. The 18 Sep OSM extract is a pre-event **road topology snapshot**, not a pre-event speed snapshot; historical same-hour flow or a matched control is needed for that second question.

Prioritize a future event with an unexpired freeze window and a confirmed independent observation feed for prospective V2/V3. For Baku, next check the organizer/AYNA for post-event *actual* operations, inspect the metro dataset when September rows appear, and locate an authorized historical road-sensor export. Keep target data out of the ontology, graph projection, policy prompts and calibration until the frozen prediction is scored.
